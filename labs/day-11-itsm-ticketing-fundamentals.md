# Day 11 – ITSM and Ticketing Fundamentals

## Learning objectives

- Understand IT Service Management (ITSM).
- Differentiate incidents, service requests and problems.
- Understand impact, urgency and priority.
- Understand SLAs and response-time targets.
- Learn the basic ticket lifecycle.

## 1. What is ITSM?

IT Service Management (ITSM) is the process of designing, delivering, managing and improving IT services.

IT support teams use ITSM practices to handle incidents, service requests and problems in a structured way.

## 2. Ticket types

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Incident | Unexpected interruption or degradation of an IT service | Outlook suddenly stops opening |
| Service Request | A standard request for a service | Requesting approved software installation |
| Problem | An underlying cause of one or more incidents | A faulty network switch causing repeated connection failures |

## 3. Priority: Impact and urgency

Priority is determined by considering the business impact and urgency of an issue.

An illustrative priority matrix:

| Impact | Urgency | Example priority |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | P1 – Critical |
| High | Medium | P2 – High |
| Medium | Medium | P3 – Normal |
| Low | Low | P4 – Low |

Actual priority definitions depend on the organisation's IT policies.

## 4. SLA fundamentals

An SLA (Service Level Agreement) defines agreed service targets.

Illustrative response-time targets:

| Priority | Response target |
|---|---|
| P1 | 15 minutes |
| P2 | 1 hour |
| P3 | 4 business hours |
| P4 | 1 business day |

**Response time** is the time taken to acknowledge or initially respond to a ticket, according to the organisation's SLA definition.

**Resolution time** is the time taken to resolve the reported issue.

Meeting a response-time target does not automatically mean the issue has been resolved.

## 5. Ticket lifecycle

1. New
2. Assigned
3. In Progress
4. Pending
5. Resolved
6. Closed

The exact workflow can vary between ticketing systems.

## 6. Practical assessment – INC-012

**Scenario:** Anna Müller from Finance reports that Outlook desktop will not open. Outlook Web works, and other employees can use Outlook desktop. The issue started that morning and she needs email for an important Finance task.

- Ticket type: Incident
- Initial priority: P3 – Normal
- Scope: Initially appears limited to one user
- Next steps: Record the exact error, establish business impact and urgency, and follow approved troubleshooting procedures.

Priority should be reviewed if the business impact or urgency changes.

## 7. Practical assessment – INC-013

**Scenario:** A P2 ticket is received at 10:00 AM and acknowledged at 10:45 AM. The response-time target is one hour.

- Actual response time: 45 minutes
- P2 response target: 60 minutes
- Result: SLA met
- Remaining time before target: 15 minutes

This assessment demonstrates the difference between response-time compliance and issue resolution.

## Key takeaways

- Correctly classify the ticket before taking action.
- Consider business impact and urgency when assigning priority.
- Record acknowledgement and resolution times accurately.
- Follow organisational procedures and escalation rules.
